Hawkish Fed hold triggers steepest selloff in months

Macro / Rates · Thursday, July 30, 2026
  • The FOMC held the federal funds rate at 3.50-3.75% for a fifth straight meeting, but the 9-3 vote split (Hammack, Kashkari and Logan dissented in favor of a 25bp hike) was read as a "hawkish hold."
  • The Dow fell 1,153.18 points (-2.19%) to 51,594.14, its worst day since April 2025; the S&P 500 slid 1.52% to 7,316.15 and the Nasdaq dropped 1.74% to 24,442.94.
  • The 10-year Treasury yield surged roughly 7-8 basis points to about 4.68%, while the 30-year yield jumped 12bps to 5.21% — its highest level since 2007.
  • The 2-year yield fell as traders priced a wider range of outcomes; for the month of July the 10-year yield is up nearly 27 basis points, the largest monthly rise since March.
  • Wall Street broadly interpreted the decision as tilting toward a possible September hike rather than the cut some had hoped for.
  • Why it matters: a Fed that stayed hawkish into an escalating Iran war and a mixed Big Tech earnings picture leaves markets with three simultaneous shocks to price at once — rates, oil and AI-capex doubt.
Sources: CNBC, Yahoo Finance, Bloomberg, TheStreet, Washington Post.
