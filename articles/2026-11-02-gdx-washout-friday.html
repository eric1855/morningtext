GDX sinks 5.21% to $77.09 Friday as gold miners lead a one-year-low washout in gold

Precious Metals / Mining Equities · Friday, October 30, 2026 (reported into the weekend)
  • The VanEck Gold Miners ETF (GDX) closed Friday at $77.09, down $4.24, or 5.21%, its sharpest one-day drop in months, as spot gold slid to its lowest close since October 2025 (around $3,877/oz, -3.7% on the week) and spot silver fell to its lowest since November 2025.
  • The move is the embedded-leverage mechanic working against EMA's book this time: producer and developer equities typically move 2-3x the metal on the way up, and Friday showed the same multiple on the way down — GDX's 5.21% loss against gold's roughly 1-2% single-day slide.
  • Monday's premarket tape shows a partial bounce: gold opened near $3,890 (vs. Friday's ~$3,899 close) before trading up toward $3,905-3,915, and silver was similarly flat-to-firmer near $49.20, suggesting Friday's miner washout may have outrun the metals themselves.
  • Why it matters to EMA: GDX is the fund's own benchmark, so Friday's print is the clearest read yet on how violently the junior/drill-story cohort's financing windows can slam shut on a single bad session — a reminder to track which names in the book actually bounced with gold Monday versus which stayed offered on the leverage unwind.
Sources: VanEck, Yahoo Finance, Finviz, Seeking Alpha, Business Standard.
