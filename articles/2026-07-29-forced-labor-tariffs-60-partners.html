New forced-labor tariffs on 60 trading partners now fully in force

Geopolitical / Trade · Wednesday, July 29, 2026
  • The USTR activated new Section 301 tariffs on 60 trading partners on July 24, replacing the temporary 10% blanket duty that had been in place, per NBC News and CNBC.
  • The new tariffs cover 99.4% of US imports: countries that have committed to enforcing bans on forced-labor goods face 10%, while 38 nations that have not — including China — face 12.5%.
  • Named countries at the 10% rate include Canada, Mexico, the UK, India, Indonesia and Bangladesh, per Troutman Pepper Locke's tracker.
  • Oil, gas, fertilizer and select food products are exempted from the new duties.
  • The action stems from USTR investigations finding each partner failed to adequately prohibit or enforce bans on forced-labor-made imports.
  • Why it matters: this replaces a temporary, more predictable blanket tariff with a permanent, country-differentiated regime — a structural shift in trade-cost assumptions for multinationals with Asian and Latin American supply chains.
Sources: NBC News, CNBC, Troutman Pepper Locke, IndexBox.
