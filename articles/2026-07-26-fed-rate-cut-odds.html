Fed minutes signal no cuts until 2027 as Iran conflict lifts hike odds

Economics / Macro · Week of July 20, 2026
  • Minutes from the Fed's June meeting show officials do not anticipate an interest-rate cut before early 2027, per Forbes.
  • The renewed Iran conflict has pushed market-implied odds of a September rate hike to nearly 70% by some measures, a sharp reversal from earlier-year cut expectations.
  • Other pricing services show September cut probability closer to 48% — the dispersion itself signals how unsettled the rate path has become as oil-driven inflation risk collides with growth concerns.
  • Goldman Sachs has also published notes arguing the Fed is unlikely to cut this year given the inflation backdrop.
  • Net for allocators: the "cuts are coming" consensus from earlier in 2026 has broken down; rate-sensitive trades (duration, small caps, housing) should be sized for a higher-for-longer scenario until the war/oil shock clears.
Sources: Forbes, Goldman Sachs Insights, rateprobability.com.
