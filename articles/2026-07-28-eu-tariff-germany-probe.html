EU-US trade deal now fully in force; new Section 301 probe targets Germany over pharma pricing

Geopolitical / Trade · Tuesday, July 28, 2026
  • The EU-US trade framework agreed earlier this year formally entered into force July 1, after approval by the European Parliament and Council of the EU, per the European Commission.
  • Terms: a 15% tariff cap on most EU exports to the US (including pharmaceuticals, semiconductors and wood products), with the EU eliminating tariffs on US industrial goods and improving market access for select US agri-food products.
  • Despite the broader deal being settled, USTR opened a separate Section 301 investigation on June 18 targeting Germany specifically, over allegedly unreasonable underpayment for innovative pharmaceutical products.
  • The EU says it has fully implemented its main commitments under the joint statement, suggesting Brussels sees the broader relationship as stabilized even as country-specific frictions persist.
  • Why it matters: the headline EU-US trade war risk has been defused, but the Germany pharma probe is a reminder that sector- and country-specific tariff escalation remains a live tail risk for EU-exposed multinationals even after a framework deal.
Sources: European Commission, Congress.gov/CRS, Baker Botts Trump Tariff Tracker.
