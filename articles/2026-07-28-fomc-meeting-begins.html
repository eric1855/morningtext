FOMC's two-day meeting begins with hike odds still near one-in-three

Macro / Rates · Tuesday, July 28, 2026
  • The Fed's July meeting runs today through tomorrow, with the decision due Wednesday afternoon; the fed funds target range has stood at 3.50-3.75% since June, per CBS News and TechTimes.
  • Odds of a 25bp hike have eased somewhat as oil retreats — pricing now clusters around 36%, down from the ~38% high last week but still well above the ~12% odds seen two weeks ago, per CME FedWatch-referenced coverage.
  • A hold remains the base case (roughly 64-79% depending on venue), with September hike odds climbing toward 80% as the more likely near-term move.
  • Chair Kevin Warsh has abandoned traditional forward guidance; his July 15 testimony reiterated an inflation-fighting stance without signaling the committee's lean.
  • Persistent inflation pressure — May CPI at 4.2% y/y on a 23.5% energy surge tied to the Iran conflict — anchors the hawkish case, while cooler June CPI/PPI and today's retreating oil price support the doves.
  • Why it matters: a still-live hike risk into Wednesday, layered on top of a deepening chip selloff and the biggest earnings week of the quarter, is a genuine source of two-way volatility the desk shouldn't discount.
Sources: CBS News, TechTimes, Forbes, Advisor Perspectives.
